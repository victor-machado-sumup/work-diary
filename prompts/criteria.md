- **Technical direction and quality**: sets or upholds technical direction,
  makes sound design decisions, documents trade-offs, keeps quality and
  maintainability high, pays attention to reliability, security and operational
  health. Bad signs: shortcuts without acknowledging the debt, decisions made
  without clear reasoning, ignoring recurring problems.
- **Code review and technical feedback**: reviews are timely, thoughtful and
  constructive; feedback explains the why and raises the bar. Bad signs:
  rubber-stamping, shallow or slow reviews, harsh or unclear comments, PRs left
  waiting.
- **Delivery and execution**: drives work to completion, breaks down work,
  unblocks the team, manages scope and risk, keeps stakeholders informed. Bad
  signs: work stalling, surprises delivered late, unclear ownership, scope
  creep left unchecked.
- **Team growth and mentoring**: coaches teammates, delegates meaningful work,
  shares context and knowledge, gives and asks for feedback, creates space for
  others to lead. Bad signs: doing everything personally, becoming a
  bottleneck, leaving teammates without context or support.
- **Collaboration and communication**: communicates clearly and asynchronously,
  aligns with other teams and product, handles disagreement constructively,
  writes things down. Bad signs: unclear messages, siloed decisions, avoiding
  difficult conversations, information only living in someone's head.
- **Ownership and initiative**: takes responsibility for outcomes, spots and
  addresses problems proactively, improves processes and tooling, follows
  through on commitments. Bad signs: waiting to be asked, dropping follow-ups,
  blaming circumstances.
- **Focus and prioritization**: spends time on what matters most, balances
  hands-on work, leadership duties and meetings, says no when needed. Bad
  signs: day consumed by low-value meetings or interruptions, context
  switching without outcomes, neglecting important-but-not-urgent work.
